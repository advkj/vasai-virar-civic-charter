THE PROBLEM IS SYSTEMIC

Basic civic services should not depend on how loudly one person complains.

Across Vasai–Virar, residents are reporting recurring problems in roads, street lighting, water supply, drainage, garbage and sanitation, electricity coordination, traffic discipline, encroachments, parking and the wider administrative response.

The Charter converts scattered WhatsApp messages and individual complaints into a structured civic record that can be presented collectively to elected representatives and competent authorities.

Our approach: evidence → official complaint → time-bound follow-up → escalation → collective representation.

01 · Document

Capture the exact location, date, photos, impact and existing complaint reference.

02 · Organise

Group similar problems by locality and civic department instead of treating every pothole as an isolated event.

03 · Escalate

Use the right statutory/administrative route and preserve the complete history.

TAKE ACTION · DON'T JUST FORWARD

Turn a civic complaint into a record.

STEP 01

Capture evidence

Photograph/video the problem. Note date, exact location and impact. Keep original files.

STEP 02

File the official complaint

Use the authority's official portal/office and retain the acknowledgement or complaint number.

STEP 03

Give a reasonable deadline

Record when the complaint was filed, when action was promised and whether the problem actually changed.

STEP 04

Escalate lawfully

Consider departmental escalation, statutory grievance mechanisms, RTI or other appropriate legal remedies depending on the issue.

STEP 05

Add it to the collective record

Submit a concise civic report here so similar problems can be grouped and represented collectively.

We are not asking citizens to break rules, obstruct roads or abuse officials. The campaign is about evidence, lawful process and accountable public administration.

Need a representation?

We can prepare a standard civic representation that groups the evidence by department, locality and urgency.

Need an RTI?

Ask for records: work orders, maintenance schedules, complaint closure details, budgets, timelines and responsible officers—where legally appropriate.

Want to help?

Share the Charter, collect accurate reports from your locality and encourage neighbours to preserve evidence rather than only forwarding messages.
